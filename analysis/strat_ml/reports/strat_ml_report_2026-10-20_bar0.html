STRAT trigger research: what the data looks like

Binance USDT-M perps, daily triggers, 2020-01-03 to 2026-10-06. Built 2026-10-20_bar0. Exploratory: every number is a hypothesis to check by eye, not a result.

How to read this page. One row of data = one time a coin broke yesterday's high (long) or low (short) on the daily chart. We pretend to enter at that level, put the stop at the far side of yesterday's bar, aim at the STRAT target, and watch 10 daily bars. R = one unit of risk (the stop distance). Win % = share of rows that ended above zero. Target first % = share that touched the target before the stop (only rows that have a target). MFE / MAE = the best and worst point reached along the way, in R. Numbers in parentheses after a win % are the 95% confidence band. Grey rows have fewer than 100 samples and should be ignored. No fees, no funding paid, no sizing: these are measuring sticks, not P&L.

The mechanical fact of this dataset: the closer the target, the more often it is touched first, and mean R barely changes. A 90% target-first rate on a 0.3R target is not an edge, it is a small target. Any claim about a pattern has to be read next to its typical reward-to-risk.

2. Clustering: let the features group themselves

What this is. K-means puts every trigger into one of 6 buckets using ONLY the 'what it looked like' columns (never the outcome). Then we look at how each bucket turned out. If some bucket is clearly better or worse, the description column says what that bucket has in common. Buckets are a lens, not a rule: the algorithm does not know what a trade is.

3. LightGBM + SHAP: which conditions move the result

What this is. A gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) is fit on triggers before 2025 and then scored on 2025-26 triggers it never saw. Model A is the money question: predict the result in R after 10 bars from the context columns only (what the week, month, 4-hour, volume, funding and BTC looked like, which pattern, how volatile, how old and how liquid the coin). It deliberately does NOT see the target distance, because a target 0.3R away is touched far more often than a target 3R away whatever the chart looks like; that is arithmetic, not edge. R-squared says how much of the row-to-row variation the model explains (0 = nothing, 1 = everything); for trading data a few hundredths is normal and the decile table is the honest read. SHAP then asks the model which columns it leaned on and in which direction.

5. Caveats that travel with every number above

  • No fees, no funding paid, fills at the level, every trigger taken, no sizing. Real trading subtracts costs from every row.
  • Ties inside one hourly bar (stop and target in the same hour) are resolved stop-first, and a stop touched in the trigger hour is counted against the trade; both are flagged in the data (about 1% of rows).
  • Targets follow the TVB-37 management-book ladder; continuation and outside breaks only have a target when a week or month level sits beyond the entry.
  • Half the coins listed in 2025-26, so the test period is dominated by young alts. Dead coins are included (survivorship handled), but a coin that died inside the 10-bar window is simply censored out.
  • With 500k rows almost every difference is 'statistically significant'. The question is never significance, it is whether the effect is big enough to survive costs and stable across years.
